# Attribution

## Methods

ProspectMap implements the Weights-of-Evidence mineral-prospectivity-mapping method and its validation machinery.
The methods are attributable to:

- **Bonham-Carter, G.F., Agterberg, F.P. & Wright, D.F. (1989).** Weights of evidence modelling: a new approach to
  mapping mineral potential. *Geol. Survey Canada Paper 89-9*, 171-183.
- **Bonham-Carter, G.F. (1994).** *Geographic Information Systems for Geoscientists: Modelling with GIS.* Pergamon,
  ch. 9 - the W+/W-/contrast, the studentized contrast, the success/prediction-rate curves, the CI tests.
- **Agterberg, F.P. & Cheng, Q. (2002).** Conditional Independence Test for Weights-of-Evidence Modeling. *Natural
  Resources Research*, 11(4), 249-255 - the new omnibus test.
- **Agterberg, F.P. & Bonham-Carter, G.F. (1990).** Deriving weights of evidence from geoscience contour maps for the
  prediction of discrete events. *22nd APCOM*, 381-395 - the WofE -> logistic-regression bridge.
- **Chung, C.-J. & Fabbri, A.G. (2003).** Validation of spatial prediction models for landslide hazard mapping.
  *Natural Hazards*, 30(3), 451-472 - the success/prediction-rate capture curves.
- **Roberts, D.R. et al. (2017).** Cross-validation strategies for data with temporal, spatial, hierarchical, or
  phylogenetic structure. *Ecography*, 40(8), 913-929 - spatial block CV / the inflation.
- **Carranza, E.J.M. (2009).** *Geochemical Anomaly and Mineral Prospectivity Mapping in GIS.* Elsevier.
- **Lawley, C.J.M. et al. (2022).** Data-driven prospectivity modelling of sediment-hosted Zn-Pb mineral systems.
  *Ore Geology Reviews*, 141, 104635 - the data release behind the REAL-USMVT case (see Data).

Full bibliography in `frontend/src/data/citations.ts` and the in-app Methodology page.

## Data

Ten study areas are **synthetic** - smooth value-noise fields (geostatistical in spirit, not variogram-controlled)
with planted per-layer weights + deposits rejection-sampled on a known latent prospectivity (fixed count per case),
generated by `frontend/src/mpm/synth.ts` + the `gen_train.mjs` sampler. The `C-NEGATIVE`, `C-CIVIOLATE`, `C-RECOVER`
and `C-SATURATE` cases are the known-ground-truth controls.

One case, **REAL-USMVT**, uses real open data, redistributed in derived form as
`data/derived/REAL-USMVT/cube.json`. It is built by `data-pipeline/pipeline/real_usmvt.py` from the Lawley et al.
(2022) Critical Minerals Mapping Initiative data release (USGS ScienceBase item 6193e9f3d34eb622f68f13a5, DOI
[10.5066/P970GDD5](https://doi.org/10.5066/P970GDD5)): four measured geophysics grids (magnetic RTP, Bouguer
gravity, depth to the lithosphere-asthenosphere boundary, satellite-gravity shape index), two proximity layers we
derive from its fault and ancient passive-margin vectors, and its Pb-Zn (MVT) occurrences, rasterized over the US
Midcontinent onto a 144 x 176 grid of about 5.4 km cells (858 deposit cells). The files used are listed in
`data/derived/REAL-USMVT/provenance.json`. The release is **US-Government public domain**; its parts sourced from
Geoscience Australia are **CC-BY 4.0**. The posterior computed on it is this tool's Weights-of-Evidence
recomputation, not the model published with the release.

Other open datasets remain candidates, each redistributable under its own licence: USGS MRDS and NURE-HSSR are
**US-Government public domain**; Geoscience Australia national geophysical grids + OZMIN are **CC-BY 4.0**; the
AlphaEarth Foundations Satellite Embedding is **CC-BY 4.0** ("produced by Google and Google DeepMind").
